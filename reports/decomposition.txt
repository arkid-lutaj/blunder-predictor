0. SANITY: is the binary flag informative, and do the floors agree?
==========================================================================
  blunder_available is true in 81.5% of positions
  frac_blunder_moves: median 0.250, p90 0.886, mean 0.344
  -> the binary flag is near-constant and carries little information.
     The decomposition therefore uses frac_blunder_moves, which is also
     exactly what uniform-random play would score.

  below the mechanical floor: 15,930 positions, max frac_blunder_moves 0.000
  labelled blunders in those rows: 6 (0.038%)
  Those are not an inconsistency. The LABEL's floor is on
  winpct_before, from Lichess' deep annotation; this floor is on
  best_child_win from a depth-8 sweep measured against the best
  child. Two different quantities, so a handful disagree.

SUMMARY
==========================================================================
  On the mechanically controlled contrast (<1200 -> 2000+),
  -1% of the drop in blunder rate runs through availability
  and 101% through selection.
  Availability 95% CI [-1.9%, +0.4%], including zero.

  That interval is NARROW and brackets zero, so this is a
  precise null rather than an absence of power: the data
  can rule out an availability channel worth more than a
  couple of points, not merely fail to detect one.

  The availability channel is essentially empty. Position
  difficulty is not something weak players walk into more
  often; they fall for it more often. That is the stronger
  claim and the one the phase was scoped to test.

  Caveat: the selection ratio s is a ratio of a measured probability
  to what UNIFORM-RANDOM play would score. Humans do not sample
  uniformly from legal moves, so s is a benchmark against random, not
  a claim about human search. A human-policy baseline (Maia) would be
  the honest denominator and is out of scope.
